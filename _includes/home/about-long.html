About

I am Anni Zhou, an Adjunct Professor of Computer Science at Northwest Nazarene University in Nampa, Idaho, where I teach Operating Systems and its lab. My research asks one question: how do we build machine-learning systems that are honest about what they don't know?

I earned my Ph.D. in Electrical and Computer Engineering at Georgia Tech in 2025, advised by Dr. Raheem Beyah and Dr. Rishikesan Kamaleswaran. My Ph.D. work was early sepsis prediction for ICU patients. I combined online learning, contextual bandits and conformal prediction, so that each prediction comes with a calibrated sense of how far it can be trusted.

I then took the same tools to agriculture. With Dr. Yuchen Liu at NC State, I built CAMP, a multi-task model that forecasts plant growth from images. It is now published in Smart Agricultural Technology.

The methods were developed in the ICU. They're not stuck there.

Next, I want to work on two things. The first is reliable large language models and AI agents: systems that can tell when they are uncertain, and act safely when they are. The second is AI for science, in healthcare and beyond, wherever a wrong prediction has a real cost.
