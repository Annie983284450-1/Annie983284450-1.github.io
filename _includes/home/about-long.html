About

I am Anni (Annie) Zhou, an Adjunct Professor of Computer Science at Northwest Nazarene University in Nampa, Idaho, where I teach Operating Systems and its lab. My research asks one question: how do we build machine-learning systems that are honest about what they don't know?

I earned my Ph.D. in Electrical and Computer Engineering at Georgia Tech in 2025, advised by Dr. Raheem Beyah and Dr. Rishikesan Kamaleswaran. My Ph.D. work was early sepsis prediction for ICU patients. I combined multi-armed bandits, online and offline, with conformal prediction, so that predictions come with a measure of how far they can be trusted.

I then moved to agriculture. With Dr. Yuchen Liu at NC State, I built CAMP, a multi-task deep learning model that forecasts plant growth from images, together with a metric that scores a forecast only on the plant pixels. It is now published in Smart Agricultural Technology.

Different data, same habit: check whether a model's output deserves to be trusted.

Next, I want to work on two things. The first is reliable large language models and AI agents: systems that can tell when they are uncertain, and act safely when they are. The second is AI for science, in healthcare and beyond, wherever a wrong prediction has a real cost.
